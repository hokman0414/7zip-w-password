Live Diamond Auctions Are TikTok Shop’s Newest Craze

Gamified livestreams are moving lab-grown stones in seconds — part of a platform that now accounts for 2% of US online retail sales.

Published

2 months ago

on

August 25, 2026

By

INSTORE Staff
  • Share
  • Tweet

Livestreamed lab-grown diamond auctions have exploded on TikTok Shop in recent weeks, according to media reports, with bidding windows as short as 10 to 15 seconds moving loose stones and finished jewelry to buyers scrolling from bed.

The Guardian, reports diamonds typically sell for $100 to $200 a carat, with many auctions opening at $1. Jeweler Kenzie Cisneros, owner of Diamond Hideout in Dallas, TX, told the paper the format has widened access to a market once reserved for wealthy buyers. One buyer cited in the piece, Andrea Greenhill of northern Mississippi, said she’s bought more than 30 diamonds through TikTok auctions since June, including a 3.78-carat stone she later had set in a ring appraised at $5,700.

Rolling Stone reports prices can run from that opening bid of $1 up to more than $2,000 for larger, higher-quality stones, though most sales fall between $100 and $500.

Much of the activity is driven by seller Royale Diamonds, which streamed a nine-hour “mega live” auction on Sunday, Mashable reported.

In an example of how gamification is being used to support sales, Rolling Stone reported one buyer accidentally won a $700 diamond via a stray tap.

How the auctions are gamified:

Advertisement
  • Countdown pressure: Bids close in as little as 10–15 seconds, with the next stone appearing immediately after
  • Low, escalating entry points: Many auctions open at $1
  • Performative hosts: Auctioneers cheerlead (“go, go, go”), needle slow bidders, and build rapport, all over club-remix soundtracks
  • Flash presentation: Stones are shown under bright lights with specs or a grading report displayed only briefly
  • Variable reward: Viewers keep watching for the anticipation of what’s next — bigger, cheaper, or a different color/cut
  • The trend sits inside a larger surge: TikTok Shop generated an estimated $50.3 billion globally in the first half of 2026, up 92% year over year, with $11.8 billion from U.S. shoppers, Mashable reported, citing Consumer Edge data. By July, the platform represented about 2% of U.S. online retail spending.

Needless to say, not everyone is sold on the quality. Kim Shaul, owner of online store Virtue Diamonds in Idaho, cautioned the Guardian that fast-paced auctions and bright lighting can mask a stone’s flaws, and that certification papers don’t guarantee good cut or light performance. While there are plenty of reputable sellers on TikTok, consumers don’t always know what to look for, Shaul explained, adding: “You can get some pretty fantastic deals on loose stones, but you might get a really garbage diamond too.”
